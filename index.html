QUESTION 1

Solve: $3x - 5 = 16$

💡 CORE CONCEPT

This question tests your ability to solve a basic two-step linear equation by isolating the variable on one side of the equation.

📝 Step-by-Step Solution

To isolate $x$, we must perform inverse operations to move the constants to the right side of the equation.

Step 1: Add $5$ to both sides of the equation to cancel out the $- 5$.

$$3x - 5 + 5 = 16 + 5$$

$$3x = 21$$

Step 2: Divide both sides by $3$ to isolate $x$.

$$\frac{3x}{3} = \frac{21}{3}$$

$$x = 7$$

Final Answer: The solution is $x = 7$.

QUESTION 2

Solve: $5x + 12 = 3x + 40$

💡 CORE CONCEPT

This question requires you to solve an equation with variables on both sides by grouping the variable terms together and the constant terms together.

📝 Step-by-Step Solution

We need to get all the $x$ terms on one side and all the numbers on the other side.

Step 1: Subtract $3x$ from both sides to group the variables on the left.

$$5x - 3x + 12 = 40$$

$$2x + 12 = 40$$

Step 2: Subtract $12$ from both sides to isolate the variable term.

$$2x = 40 - 12$$

$$2x = 28$$

Step 3: Divide both sides by $2$.

$$\frac{2x}{2} = \frac{28}{2}$$

$$x = 14$$

Final Answer: The solution is $x = 14$.

QUESTION 40

💡 CORE CONCEPT

This is a "Difference of Squares" pattern. It takes the form $a^2 - b^2$, which always factors into the two binomials $(a + b)(a - b)$.

📝 Step-by-Step Solution

We identify the square roots of the two terms and plug them into the pattern.

Step 1: Find the square root of the first term.

$$\sqrt{x^2} = x$$

Step 2: Find the square root of the second term.

$$\sqrt{9} = 3$$

Step 3: Write the two binomial factors, one with a plus and one with a minus.

$$(x + 3)(x - 3)$$

Final Answer: The solution is $(x + 3)(x - 3)$.

QUESTION 41

💡 CORE CONCEPT

Factoring by finding the Greatest Common Factor (GCF) involves identifying the largest variable or number that evenly divides into all terms of an expression.

📝 Step-by-Step Solution

We will look for the common element shared by both $x^2$ and $5x$.

Step 1: Identify the GCF. Both terms share an $x$.

Step 2: Divide both terms by $x$ to see what remains inside the parentheses.

$$\frac{x^2}{x} = x$$

$$\frac{5x}{x} = 5$$

Step 3: Write the GCF outside the parentheses and the remaining parts inside.

$$x(x + 5)$$

Final Answer: The solution is $x(x + 5)$.

QUESTION 42

System of equations concept: Which point satisfies both equations?

💡 CORE CONCEPT

The solution to a system of equations is the coordinate point $(x, y)$ where the two lines intersect. This point must make both equations mathematically true when substituted.

📝 Step-by-Step Solution

If given a multiple-choice list of points, the most reliable method is to plug the $x$ and $y$ values into both equations to check for equality.

Step 1: Take the $(x, y)$ coordinate from the first option.

Step 2: Substitute $x$ and $y$ into the first equation. If the left side equals the right side, proceed. If not, eliminate the option.

Step 3: Substitute the same $x$ and $y$ into the second equation. If it is also true, you have found the solution.

Final Answer: The solution is the specific point that satisfies both equations simultaneously.

QUESTION 17

A cylinder has radius $4\text{ cm}$ and height $10\text{ cm}$. Find volume.

💡 CORE CONCEPT

The volume of a cylinder is found by calculating the area of its circular base and multiplying it by its height. The formula is $V = \pi r^2 h$.

📝 Step-by-Step Solution

We will substitute $r = 4$ and $h = 10$ into the volume formula.

Step 1: Substitute the values into the formula.

$$V = \pi(4)^2(10)$$

Step 2: Square the radius.

$$V = \pi(16)(10)$$

Step 3: Multiply the numbers to find the exact volume in terms of $\pi$.

$$V = 160\pi$$

Step 4: For an approximate decimal value, multiply by $3.14$.

$$V \approx 160 \times 3.14 = 502.4$$

Final Answer: The solution is $160\pi\text{ cm}^3$ (or approximately $502.4\text{ cm}^3$).

QUESTION 5

Find the slope between $(2, 4)$ and $(6, 12)$.

💡 CORE CONCEPT

The slope of a line represents its steepness and is calculated as the ratio of the change in the y-values (rise) to the change in the x-values (run) between two points.

📝 Step-by-Step Solution

We will use the standard slope formula: $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$. Let $(x_1, y_1) = (2, 4)$ and $(x_2, y_2) = (6, 12)$.

Step 1: Substitute the coordinates into the formula.

$$m = \frac{12 - 4}{6 - 2}$$

Step 2: Simplify the numerator and the denominator.

$$m = \frac{8}{4}$$

Step 3: Divide to find the final value.

$$m = 2$$

Final Answer: The solution is $m = 2$.

QUESTION 26

Identify if the table represents a linear relationship.

💡 CORE CONCEPT

A linear relationship has a constant rate of change. This means that as the x-values increase by a steady amount, the y-values must also increase or decrease by a steady, consistent amount.

📝 Step-by-Step Solution

To check if a table is linear, calculate the change in $y$ divided by the change in $x$ for every set of points.

Step 1: Look at the changes in the x-values. Do they increase by the same amount?

Step 2: Look at the changes in the y-values. Do they increase or decrease by the same amount?

Step 3: If the ratio of the change in $y$ to the change in $x$ ($\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$) is exactly the same for every pair, it is linear. If the rate changes, it is non-linear.

Final Answer: The solution is verified by checking if the rate of change (slope) remains constant.

QUESTION 46

Identify function from mapping diagram (concept).

💡 CORE CONCEPT

For a relationship to be a true mathematical function, every single input (x-value) must point to exactly one unique output (y-value). If an input points to two different outputs, it is not a function.

📝 Step-by-Step Solution

When looking at a mapping diagram, you analyze the arrows leaving the input domain.

Step 1: Look at each number in the "Input" or "x" circle.

Step 2: Count how many arrows leave each input number.

Step 3: If any input number has two or more arrows leaving it, the relationship is NOT a function. If every input has only one arrow leaving it, it IS a function.

Final Answer: The solution depends on ensuring no x-value repeats with a different y-value.

$$Density = 5~g/cm^3$$

QUESTION 1

What is the main role of the legislative branch of the US government?

💡 CORE CONCEPT

The main role of the legislative branch, which consists of the House of Representatives and the Senate (collectively known as Congress), is to make the laws.

📜 Context & Explanation

Historical Background / Context: When the US Constitution was written, the framers wanted to ensure that the power to create laws rested directly with the representatives of the people, rather than a king. They created a bicameral (two-house) legislature: the House (based on population) and the Senate (two per state).

Why It Matters Today: This system ensures that laws regarding everything from taxes to healthcare are debated and voted on by elected officials representing local and state interests.

How does the judicial branch check the executive branch?

💡 CORE CONCEPT

The judicial branch checks the executive branch by holding the power to declare executive actions or orders unconstitutional.

📜 Context & Explanation

Historical Background / Context: In the landmark 1803 Supreme Court case *Marbury v. Madison*, the Court established the principle of "judicial review." This means the Supreme Court has the final say on whether an action taken by the President (Executive Branch) or a law passed by Congress violates the rules set in the US Constitution.

Why It Matters Today: This prevents a President from acting like a dictator by issuing orders that violate citizens' constitutional rights.

💡 CORE CONCEPT

The three branches of the US government are the Legislative, Executive, and Judicial branches.

📜 Context & Explanation

Historical Background / Context: Influenced by Enlightenment thinkers like Montesquieu, the founding fathers designed a government with a "separation of powers." Instead of one ruler holding all power, power is split. Why It Matters Today: This division prevents any single group or individual from gaining total control over the United States.

QUESTION 4

What is the purpose of checks and balances?

💡 CORE CONCEPT

The system of checks and balances ensures that no single branch of government becomes too powerful by giving each branch the ability to limit (check) the powers of the other two.

📜 Context & Explanation

Historical Background / Context: The Constitution was designed so the branches are interlocked. For example, Congress passes a law, but the President can veto it. However, Congress can override that veto with a 2/3 majority. The Supreme Court can then declare that law unconstitutional, but the President appoints the Supreme Court justices, and Congress must approve those appointments.

Why It Matters Today: It ensures cooperation, prevents tyranny, and forces compromise in American politics.

Why It Matters Today: It explains why laws regarding things like marijuana legalization or driving ages can differ drastically from one state to another, while military and border laws apply to the whole country.

QUESTION 28

Describe the election process in the US.

💡 CORE CONCEPT

The US election process for the President is an indirect system where citizens vote for "electors" in the Electoral College, rather than directly electing the candidate by popular vote.

📜 Context & Explanation

Historical Background / Context: The framers of the Constitution created the Electoral College as a compromise. Each state gets a certain number of electoral votes based on its total representation in Congress (House + Senate). To win the presidency, a candidate must secure a majority of these electoral votes (currently 270 out of 538). In almost every state, if a candidate wins the popular vote in that state, they get *all* of its electoral votes.

Why It Matters Today: Because of this system, it is mathematically possible for a candidate to win the national popular vote but lose the presidency if they don't win the right combination of states.

Historical Background / Context: When reading demographic data, voting results, or economic outputs across different states or countries, you need to quickly see who has the most and who has the least. Bar graphs place categories on one axis and the numerical amount on the other, making comparisons instantaneous.

Why It Matters Today: It is the easiest way to visually compare differences between discrete groups without getting lost in spreadsheets.

QUESTION 22

What is a line graph used for?

💡 CORE CONCEPT

A line graph is specifically used to track changes or trends in data over a continuous period of time.

📜 Context & Explanation

Historical Background / Context: While a bar graph compares different items at one moment, a line graph looks at *one* item as it moves through time (days, months, years). If you want to know if unemployment went up or down during the 1930s, a line graph instantly shows the trend via the slope of the line.

Why It Matters Today: Line graphs are essential for spotting economic trends, historical population changes, and forecasting the future.

QUESTION 34

Human rights are the basic, universal rights and freedoms that inherently belong to every person in the world, simply by virtue of being human.

📜 Context & Explanation

Historical Background / Context: Following the atrocities of WWII, the United Nations adopted the Universal Declaration of Human Rights in 1948. Why It Matters Today: When governments violate these rights (such as using torture or denying freedom of speech), the international community can use sanctions or intervention to protect the oppressed.

QUESTION 42

What is the difference between direct and representative democracy?

💡 CORE CONCEPT

In a direct democracy, citizens vote directly on every single law and policy, while in a representative democracy (like the US), citizens elect politicians to make those decisions on their behalf.

📜 Context & Explanation

Historical Background / Context: Ancient Athens practiced direct democracy, where all eligible citizens gathered to vote on every issue. As nations grew massive, this became physically impossible. The US was founded as a representative democracy (or a Republic). We elect Senators and Representatives whose full-time job is to study issues and vote on laws for us.

Why It Matters Today: Representative democracy allows for a massive nation to function efficiently, though citizens must hold their representatives accountable during elections to ensure their voices are still heard.
