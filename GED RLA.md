---

Rule 1: Absolute or All-or-Nothing Words

Look for answer choices containing:

- Always
- Never
- Everyone
- Nobody
- All
- None
- Must
- Only

Explanation:

GED reading passages rarely provide enough evidence to prove something happens all the time.

Correct answers are often more moderate and use words like:

- Some
- Many
- Often
- Typically
- Generally

Example:

Bad answer:

"Everyone benefits from exercise."

Better answer:

"Many people benefit from exercise."

---

Rule 2: Emotional or Extreme Language

Look for answer choices containing words like:

- Amazing
- Disgusting
- Hateful
- Perfect
- Ridiculous
- Failure

Explanation:

GED authors usually write in a neutral and academic tone.

Correct answers typically summarize information objectively.

Example:

Bad answer:

"The policy was a complete disaster."

Better answer:

"The policy had several unintended consequences."

---

Rule 3: Unsupported Comparisons

Watch for phrases such as:

- Better than
- Worse than
- More than
- Less than
- Most effective
- Least effective

Explanation:

Only choose comparative answers when the passage directly compares the items.

Example:

Bad answer:

"Solar energy is better than wind energy."

Better answer:

"The passage explains advantages of solar energy."

---

Rule 4: Half-Right, All-Wrong

Explanation:

An answer choice may begin correctly but include one incorrect detail.

If any part of an answer is inaccurate, eliminate it.

Example:

Passage says:

"The city expanded parks and improved public transportation."

Wrong answer:

"The city expanded parks and lowered taxes."

The first half is correct, but the second half is unsupported.

Therefore the entire answer is incorrect.

---

Question Format

For each question:

1. Provide a short reading passage.
2. Ask a GED-style question.
3. Provide four answer choices (A, B, C, D).
4. Include one correct answer.
5. Include realistic distractors.
6. Ensure distractors demonstrate the specific elimination trap being taught.

Example:

Passage:

"Many students reported that participating in study groups helped them understand difficult material."

Question:

Which statement is best supported by the passage?

A. All students learn better in study groups.
B. Many students found study groups helpful.
C. Study groups are the only effective learning method.
D. Students never learn well alone.

Correct Answer:

B

Explanation:

A uses an absolute word ("all").

C uses an absolute word ("only").

D uses an absolute word ("never").

B accurately reflects the passage.
